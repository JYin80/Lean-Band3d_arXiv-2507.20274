## 2. B — LW-12e = T2264 (certificate route)

### 2.1 Are the claims about the paper right? Yes, both. They are gaps in the write-up; the lemma stands.
**(a) The pigeonhole (`7_8:1388-1393`).**
- The induction runs over graphs whose paths pass through external vertices. The paper says so itself (`:1161`, "some path may pass through them"), after fixing `α_q` as external in cases (I)/(II).
- The count "2p ending edges, at most p B2, hence at least p A1/B1 edges at internal vertices" assumes that every ending edge has an internal endpoint.
- An ending edge between two external vertices has none. `figIVext` is an example: `q = 1 < p = 2`, edge `a₁b₀`, in case (IV), confirmed by the preflight's `inst.py`.
- So properties (1)–(2) do not follow, and `(kwuyayw_ng)` is reached with a weaker structure than the paper assumes.

**(b) The re-rooting (iii) (`:1446-1447`).**
- If `α₁α₂` is a bridge of the component, every spanning tree contains it. With root `α₁`, the vertex `α₂` is then always a child of `α₁`, and (iii) cannot produce `𝕋'`.
- In the `(ξ_{α₁a₁})²` branch, `α₂` reaches its turn with only the tree edge to `α₁` (its other internal edges were used in its subtree). Cauchy–Schwarz then has one edge, not two.
- The fix is to take the AM-GM pair on an edge that is not a bridge. One exists: every vertex has at least two internal edges, so the component has at least `r` edges, hence a cycle or a parallel pair. This is exactly `anpKey5_graph`'s step.
