# lem:localregular property (6), `ord(Γ_{μ,xy}) ≥ 2p`: invariant search for LW-10c (2026-10-04)

Scope: mathematics only, on the merged Lean `LocStep` (`LWLvl1.lean:3260-3275`: weight / edge / GG expansions, each
followed by the dotted-edge partition `LWVocab.lean:1303`), via my own Python model `2026-10-04-localreg6-model.py`
(this directory; runner `2026-10-04-localreg6-verify.py`, output `2026-10-04-localreg6-output.txt`, recorded path
`2026-10-04-localreg6-cexpath.txt`). `ord = n_S + 2(n_W − n_V)`; "far" = `M_x ≠ M_y` (eq:far_ab, `7_8:792`).

## 0. Verdict

* **No counterexample to (6).** Exhaustively (p = 2, every far state with ord ≤ 4 expanded, 11,233,904 far
  outputs) no far locally standard output has ord ≤ 4 and none with ord 5 was produced; the least seen is 6 = 3p
  (3p-1 = 5 is not excluded by this run alone).  In all samples at p = 4 the least is 12 = 3p.  (6) is numerically
  solid and, as the paper's remark `B:280-283` says, the far bound is probably 3p.  No route-level problem.
* **The paper's bookkeeping is wrong, the mechanism behind it is right once stated correctly.** The quantity that
  the dynamics conserve is not `ord + n_dv + n_lw` but the *resolution cost* of §2: the ord that the cheapest future
  sequence of free/cheap steps (`R2` at a same-colour vertex, merges of the partition) can still reach.  Three
  corrections to the paper's "distinguished vertex" count are forced by the step table (§2.2): monochromatic cycles of
  such vertices cost 2 less, chains closing on one vertex cost 1 less, and vertex merges close every chain between the
  merged vertices at once.  Each of these is realized by concrete reachable graphs (§5).
* **Proof route (open, not proved):** the invariant `Ψ_min(Q) := min over far partitions π of V(Q) of
  [ord + rescost](M_π Q) ≥ 3p` (§3). Its step lemma reduces, by one general reduction (§4.1-4.2), to a *local lemma*
  about one term applied to an arbitrary pre-merged graph (§4.3), plus the initial computation `Ψ_min(Γ_p) = 3p`
  (proved, §4.4).  The local lemma is numerically verified on 0.86 M (container) + 0.34 M (device) random instances
  with no counterexample, but it has no hand proof; it is the research risk of LW-10c.  Two explicit potentials were
  found and refuted (`Ψ`, exact resolution cost: reachable counterexample, §2.3) or survive every test but are not
  the right object (`Ψ₃`, §3).
* Recommendation (§7): state (6) as `ord ≥ 2p` for **every** output (far and not), derived from the same invariant with
  the far restriction dropped (`Ψ_min^all(Γ_p) = 2p`, §4.4); this removes the (scalemole) error-term reduction of §47.

## 1. The model (what was checked against the Lean definitions)

Vertices x, y external; solid edge = (colour, circled, src, dst); loops are light-weights (`circ`) or weights.

## 2. The mechanism: half-edges, elementary vertices, chains

For an internal vertex v let `H(v)` be the multiset of half-edges (colour, in/out) of its non-loop edges, a loop of
colour c counting as one c-in and one c-out.  v is **elementary** if `H(v) = {c-in, c-out}` for one colour c:
either an **SC vertex** (two non-loop same-colour edges, one in, one out: the paper's distinguished vertex, and the
only vertex type the GG step acts on) or a **lone light-weight** (`L`).  An SC vertex v of colour c has
`pred(v) → v → nxt(v)`; maximal sequences of SC vertices along `nxt` are **chains**: *open* (entry vertex ≠ exit
vertex, both non-SC), *closed on one vertex* (`cyc1`: entry = exit, external or non-SC internal), or *cycles*.

**2.1 Resolution cost.**  The GG term `R2` at an SC vertex (`G_{y'y}` + `S⁺_{xy}`, weight dropped) has
`Δord = +1`, except `Δord = 0` when `y = y'` (the weight `G_{yy}` is dropped).  Resolving a whole chain by `R2`s
therefore costs k (open, k vertices), k − 1 (`cyc1`: the last step closes a 2-cycle on the entry vertex),
k − 2 (cycle).  With `rescost := |L| + Σ_chains k − 2·#cycles − #cyc1`, the quantity
`Ψ := ord + rescost` is `ord(R(Q)) + |L(Q)|`, the ord after all SC vertices are resolved for free.
`Ψ(Γ_p) = p + p + p = 3p`.  (Why `|L|` counts 1: `T3` at a lone light-weight with a same-colour edge `q` costs
`Δord = 1` and converts the light-weight into two SC vertices of a chain; the paper's `n_lw` is right.)

**2.2 Why the paper's `ord + n_dv + n_lw` is not monotone.**  Observed minima of `ΔΨ_exact` per unmerged term
(all weights dropped; `same`/`opp` = colour of the hit edge `q` relative to the expanded edge; p = 2 sample):
`T1 T2 Oe1xOwx R3 R4 R5 R6: +2`; `P5 P6: +1`; `T3 same: +1 (0 if q is a light-weight)`, `T3 opp: 0`,
`T4 same: +2 (0 if q is a light-weight)`, `T4 opp: 0`, `D: 0`, `P3 P4: 0`, `R2: 0`, `R7/R8 same or opp: 0`.
The zero cases are exactly where a same-colour cycle or closure appears (`R2` on a 2-cycle: `Δord = 0`, two SC vertices gone), or where an opposite-colour edge is
pulled (the paper's case (i)/(vi) with `Δord = 0` after the m-branch of a new weight, `T2151b`).  An LP over all
half-edge pattern counts shows: without the cycle and closure corrections **no** potential of the form
`ord + Σ c_k·(pattern counts)` with `c ≥ 0` is monotone on the data except `ord` itself (`max D(Γ_2) = 0`); with
them the LP optimum is `+1` per elementary vertex, `−2` per cycle, `−1` per `cyc1` (= `Ψ`).

**2.3 Merges.**  Merging two classes `u, v` (at least one internal) in the partition drops the j new edges between
them and gives `Δord = 2 − j` (`j ≤ 2`, by normality), and turns every chain from u to v into a `cyc1`
(`Δrescost = −1` each) — so `ΔΨ = 2 − j − m` for m such chains.  With m ≥ 3 this is negative.  Reachable
instance (`2026-10-04-localreg6-cexpath.txt`, verified step by step by `checkpath`): 17 steps from `Γ_2` reach a far
graph whose internal vertex `3` carries a light-weight and is joined to `x` by one direct edge `3 → x` and by three
single-SC chains (`x → 2 → 3`, `3 → 4 → x`, `x → 5 → 3`); the next `T3` (the light-weight at `3` hits the direct edge)
merges `3` into `x` through the new vertex α (the three chains close on x) and gives `Ψ 65 → 64` while
`ord 62 → 64`.  Hence **`Ψ_exact` (and any per-vertex marking of the paper's kind) is not an
invariant.**  Note `Ψ = 65 ≫ 3p = 6` there: the loss is paid from slack created earlier by the merges that built the
parallel chains, which is precisely what `Ψ_min` below records.

## 3. The invariants

* **`Ψ`** (exact resolution cost, §2.1): `≥ 3p` on every far state seen (minimum 6 at p = 2 on all 1295 far states
  with ord ≤ 4 and on 10 M sampled outputs); **not monotone** (§2.3).
* **`Ψ_loc`** (`ord + |L| +` maximal number of SC vertices markable with the local rules "`nxt v` unmarked" and
  "`pred v ≠ nxt v`"): monotone on everything except the adversarial parallel-chain searches (same counterexample).
* **`Ψ₃`** `:= Ψ − Σ_{a≠b} (m(a,b) − 2)₊`, m(a,b) = number of open chains with end-vertex set {a,b}: anticipates
  the merge of §2.3.  `Ψ₃(Γ_p) = 2p + 2`.  **0 violations on all 30 M outputs** including the adversarial searches
  that break `Ψ` (§5), but it is a heuristic: a merge that joins two vertices sharing a third partner with ≥ 2
  chains each can lose 2 more than it gains, and I could neither reach nor exclude such a configuration.
* **`Ψ_min`** (recommended): `Ψ_min^far(Q) := min_{π far} [ord(M_π Q) + rescost(M_π Q)]`, the min over all
  partitions π of V(Q) with x, y in different classes, where `M_π Q` merges each class to one vertex, keeps
  circled loops and deletes every other solid edge inside a class.  `Ψ_min^all` is the same without the far
  restriction.  `Ψ_min ≤ Ψ` (trivial partition).  Invariant: `Ψ_min^far(Q) ≥ 3p` (resp. `Ψ_min^all(Q) ≥ 2p`).

## 4. Proof route for `Ψ_min` (what is proved, what is the open local lemma)

**4.1 Lemma A (dropping a loop never helps).**  For any graph X and any loop ℓ at a vertex v, with X⁻ = X minus
ℓ (so `n_S − 1`): `Ψ(X⁻) ≤ Ψ(X)`.  Proof by the pattern of v after removal: `H(v) = ∅` (v was a lone
light-weight: `ΔΨ = −2`); `H(v) = {c-in, c-out}` with c the colour of ℓ or the other colour (v becomes SC and joins
the two chain pieces at v: `Δrescost ∈ {0, +1}`, `ΔΨ ∈ {−1, 0}`); any other pattern or v external (`ΔΨ = −1`).
Hence for every output the worst case is "every new weight dropped", and `Ψ_min(Q') ≥ Ψ_min(T)` for the unmerged
term T with all its weights dropped, because a partition π' of the output pulls back to a partition of T whose
merged graph differs from `M_{π'} Q'` only by kept weights (Lemma A), and `far` pulls back to `far`.  [proved]

**4.2 Reduction.**  So the step lemma `Ψ_min(Q') ≥ Ψ_min(Q)` follows from: *for every far partition π of V(T)
there is a far partition π₀ of V(Q) with `cost(M_{π₀} Q) ≤ cost(M_π T)`*, `cost := ord + rescost`.  The Lean
partition's own merges (which pairs, through which new vertices, consistency) never have to be analysed: they are
absorbed in the min.  [proved, given 4.3]

**4.3 Local lemma (open).**  Take `π₀ := π|V(Q)` ("naive").  Numerically (`premerge` mode, §5): the naive
inequality holds in **all** 0.49 M instances where every new vertex α/β is placed alone or into a class containing
one of its neighbours along a new edge (the only placements the dotted-edge partition can produce), and fails only
for unrealizable placements (α put into an unrelated class; 39 of 0.71 M such instances, 0.005 %), and in every such
failure one extra merge in π₀ repairs it.  So the lemma to prove is: `cost(M_{π₀} Q) ≤ cost(M_π T)` for π₀ = the
restriction of π, possibly coarsened by one more merge.  Its content is a finite case analysis on classes
`[w], [a], [b], [α]` (resp. `[x], [v]`, `[x], [y], [y'], [α], [β]`), the colour of `q`, coincidences among the
classes, and the chain pieces at those classes: the quantities in §2.2 recomputed for an arbitrary multigraph.
The merge formula of §2.3 is the special case "two classes".  Cases with the largest number of sub-cases: `T3`,
`T4`, `R7`, `R8` (derivative hits `q`), `D`.  The exact `ΔΨ` tables for the unmerged terms (§2.2) and for
single merges (§2.3: `(1)` u, v non-SC: `2 − j − m − [u∈L] − [v∈L]`; `(2)` u SC, v not: `+1` or `−1` (v adjacent
to u in an open chain) or `−1` (v the closing vertex of u's `cyc1` chain, u interior); `(3)` u, v SC: `0`
(consecutive), `−1` (same open chain, not consecutive), `≥ 0` otherwise) are the expected sub-results.

**4.4 Initial values [proved].**  For `Γ_p` and any far partition: with `d_ext` = α's merged into the classes of
x or y, and internal classes = s singletons {α_i}, ℓ singletons {β_i}, r classes of size ≥ 2 (every solid edge of
`Γ_p` touches x or y, so internal classes contain no solid edge), `cost − 3p = 2p − s − ℓ − 2r − d_ext ≥ 0` since
`2p ≥ d_ext + s + ℓ + 2r`.  So `Ψ_min^far(Γ_p) = 3p` (trivial partition).  Allowing x = y, the partition {x, y, all
α_i} ∪ {β_i} singletons costs `p + p = 2p` and brute force (`p = 2, 3`: 203 / 4140 partitions) gives
`Ψ_min^all(Γ_p) = 2p`.  **Conclusion** at a locally standard output (no loops, no SC vertex, `rescost = 0` for the
trivial partition): `ord ≥ Ψ_min ≥ 3p` (far) resp. `≥ 2p` (all).  This is where (eq:far_ab) enters: closing the
p initial chains `x → α_i → y` on x = y is the one merge that costs p.

**4.5 Where the step lemma is tight (the cases a Lean proof must get exactly right).**  Unmerged, weights dropped,
colours relative to the expanded edge; `ΔΨ_exact` is the sum of `Δord` and `Δrescost`:
* `R2` (`Δord +1`, one SC vertex of a chain removed, chain type unchanged): `0` — this *is* the resolution step.
* `T3 opp` generic (`Δord +1`): the new α is standard (`b-out`, `r-out`); w gains `{r-in, b-in}`, so a lone
  light-weight w becomes standard (`−1`); the red chain through `a → b` is cut (`−1` only if its entry is w): `≥ 0`.
* `T3 same` with `q` a light-weight at b (`Δord +1`): two light-weights consumed (`−2`), the new chain
  `b → α → w → b` is a `cyc1` on b or a cycle (`+1`): `≥ 0`.  Generic `T3 same`: α (and a lone w) are inserted into
  the chain of `q`, `≥ +1`.  `a = w`: α closes on w (cost 0), `+1`.  `b = w` (weight `G_ww` dropped, `Δord 0`):
  α is a new open chain, `≥ +1`.
* `D`, `P3`, `P4` (edge step; `Δord +1, 0, 0`): `P3` moves a standard pair, `P4` an SC pair from x to α
  (`Δrescost 0`); `D` creates an SC α while cutting the chain of `q` (two possible closures): `≥ 0`.
* `R7`, `R8` (`Δord +1`): the splice `… y' → x → b …` / `… a → α → y …` creates α (`+1`) and at most two closures
  (both only when the chain through x and the chain of `q'` connect the same two vertices in opposite directions):
  `≥ 0`.  `T4 opp` behaves like `T3 opp`; `T1 T2 R3-R6 P5 P6 Oe1xOwx` have slack `≥ 1`.
* Merges (§4.3 (1)-(3)): the `−1` cases (SC vertex merged with the neighbour/closing vertex of its chain; two
  non-consecutive SC vertices of one open chain; §2.3 with `j + m ≥ 3`) were always paid by the unmerged slack
  (attribution over 3 M outputs: no output with total `ΔΨ_exact < 0` except the parallel-chain family of §2.3).
  With `Ψ_min` none of this has to be proved: the min over π absorbs every merge.

**4.6 Property (4).**  Unchanged from T2151 (Inv4, 0 failures); independent of (6).

## 6. Outputs with `M_x = M_y`

(scalemole) `7_8:191` gives, up to `exp(−c(log W)^{3/2})`, `|x − y| ≤ W(log W)^{3/2}` when x, y share a molecule,
and (eq:far_ab) `|a − b| > (log W)^{3/2}` in block units is `|x − y| > W(log W)^{3/2}`; so every output with
`M_x = M_y` is an error term of size `exp(−c(log W)^{3/2})·(trivial bound)` — but only through an analytic decay
estimate for waved-edge paths that, as far as I can see, has no Lean counterpart (`lvl1_lemma_size` counts, it
does not localise).
The numerics (T2151 and here) say the detour is unnecessary: the least ord of a locally standard output with
`M_x = M_y` is 4 = 2p (p = 2, exhaustive over all states with ord ≤ 3) and 8 = 2p (p = 4, beams), and §4.4 explains
it: `Ψ_min^all(Γ_p) = 2p`.  **LW-10c should state `LocReg6 : 2p ≤ ord` for every `Q ∈ outs`** (the statement
`PGraph.LocReg6` already has this form), from the single invariant `Ψ_min^all ≥ 2p`, and may add the far statement
`M_x ≠ M_y → 3p ≤ ord` from `Ψ_min^far ≥ 3p` as a corollary of the same step lemma.  If the dispatcher keeps §47's
far-only (6), the error-term reduction still needs a new analytic lemma (decay of `S`, `S^±` along a waved path
between x and y) — not a graph-counting statement, and not covered by anything merged.

## 8. Where the model may differ from `LocStep`

(1) Twists: every (c, t) is enumerated; Lean's relation also allows both, so the invariant is checked for the
superset. (2) Dotted edges are stored implicitly as the solid pairs of the parent (`Normal` (iii)); duplicate ×
edges after merges are collapsed (same output graphs, different multiplicities). (3) Coefficients, `R1`, `m 1_{x=y₁}`
ignored (as in Lean). (4) Waved loops after merges are kept (as `WEdge.map`). (5) Edge-step hypotheses `hbad`,
`hwf`, GG `hnb` follow `LWLvl1:3266-3274`; `Lvl1Reach`'s cutoff and "not locally standard" filters are not applied
(superset again). (6) Nothing was proved from Lean; all fidelity statements are those of T2142 (a) plus the counts
above.
